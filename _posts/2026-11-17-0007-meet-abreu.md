## The short version

Findings has a fourth model line. **Abreu** reads a frontal chest radiograph
and scores fourteen findings at once instead of one. Seven of them are the
ones the project will try to put into report text: pleural effusion,
pneumothorax, cardiomegaly, edema, consolidation, nodule and mass. It was
trained on exactly the same patients, splits and recipe as Damoiseau, the
single-finding effusion model, so the two can be compared image by image. The
first run answered one question and left another open. On effusion the
comparison came out **silent**: Abreu is neither clearly as good as Damoiseau
nor clearly worse. On the other six it produced the project's first baselines.
Four findings are usable on both held-out sets, one is usable on one set only,
and nodule is weak.

## What changed

### Damoiseau came back, and then froze

Entry 0004 paused Damoiseau at AUROC 0.879, trained on a fifth of the NIH
ChestX-ray14 dataset. Entry 0006 promised its return. In early October it got
the run it had been waiting for: version 2, trained on all 112,120 images. Only
the amount of data changed.

Comparing v2 with v1 needed a trick. The obvious comparison is invalid. The two
versions drew different patients, so the only thing the v1-versus-v2 contrast
can measure is two models on two different exams, which is a difference in
sample as much as in model. The fix was to take every patient from v1's test
set and set them aside as a **reserve** before v2's splits were drawn. That way
neither model has ever seen them, both can be scored on the same images, and
the difference is paired. On that reserve, v2 beat v1 by **+0.0102 AUROC**
(95% CI by patient, +0.0007 to +0.0194). On its own, much larger test set, v2
scored 0.8796, which happens to be the same level as v1's 0.8792. "It went from
0.879 to 0.889" is a sentence this project now forbids. It mixes a level
measured on one set with a level measured on another.

Then a second training seed, run under a rule written down before it ran. On
the reserve, the two seeds agree to within 0.001, so the gain belongs to the
recipe and not to luck. On the own test set, the second seed is **0.0068
lower** than the first, with an interval that excludes zero. Two runs of the
identical recipe differ by two thirds of the gain the recipe bought. That
number became the ruler for everything after it, including this entry.

After that, Damoiseau stopped. Its v2 checkpoint is frozen as the reference for
effusion. A planned v3 at 448 pixels was archived before it ran.

### Why a new line instead of v3

The free GPU quota does not fit two chest models, and it barely notices the
difference between one output and fourteen: the backbone does nearly all of the
work, and a wider head is almost free. A model that can only say "effusion" can
write one sentence of a chest report. A radiologist reading a chest film is
looking for a dozen things. So the next model reads the dozen, and Damoiseau's
role shrinks to the control it is now best placed to be.

### The name

Manoel de Abreu was a Brazilian physician who, in 1936, worked out how to
photograph the fluoroscopy screen onto small film. That made a chest
radiograph fast and cheap enough to take of entire populations, and it became
the standard way to screen for tuberculosis for decades. In Brazil the exam
still carries his name: *abreugrafia*.

That breaks this project's naming rule, for the second time. Damoiseau and
Segond are named after signs you can see in an image. Greulich is named after
an atlas. Abreu is named after a person. The rule now reads: a sign, or a
person or reference that reading this kind of image owes something to. For a
model whose job is to read the whole chest, the man who made reading the whole
chest routine seemed a better fit than any single sign.

### What changes, and what stays

The comparison only means something if almost nothing moves, so the list of
changes is short:

- **The head:** one output becomes fourteen, one for each NIH label.
- **The loss:** an average of fourteen binary losses, each weighted by its own
  class imbalance, capped at 100. At NIH prevalences the cap only bites on
  hernia.
- **Checkpoint selection:** by the mean validation AUROC of the seven headline
  findings. Selecting by effusion would have tilted the effusion verdict
  towards Abreu before it was read.

Everything else is identical to Damoiseau v2: the splits, the reserve, the
seed, `convnext_tiny` with ImageNet weights, 224 pixels, five epochs, batch
size, learning rate, no flipping. Before the first epoch, the run aborts unless
its four split files match v2's line by line and its effusion column matches
v2's label column exactly. Without that, the paired comparison is a
comparison of two datasets.

### Two rulers, written before the run

Effusion is read **paired**, against both Damoiseau seeds, on both the own test
set and the reserve. The non-inferiority margin is −0.010, which is the size of
the whole gain from v1 to v2. Losing more than that would undo everything the
full dataset bought.

The other six have no control: no earlier model on this partition was ever
trained on them. So they get **baselines**, read against fixed lines. A lower
confidence bound of at least 0.75 counts as *usable*, and anything below that
is *weak*. Any AUROC at or above 0.95 is treated as suspicious and checked for
leakage before it is reported. The 0.75 sits just under the weakest of these
six in the published CheXNet results. That is a sanity check, not a
comparison, because the partitions are different.

## Numbers

**Effusion, paired.** Δ AUROC = Abreu − control, 95% CI by patient
bootstrap.

| Control | Own test | Reserve |
|---|---|---|
| Damoiseau v2 | −0.0072 [−0.0120, −0.0023] | +0.0021 [−0.0055, +0.0100] |
| Damoiseau v2, second seed | −0.0003 [−0.0053, +0.0049] | +0.0012 [−0.0078, +0.0107] |

Abreu's effusion AUROC: 0.872 on the own test set, 0.892 on the reserve.
Verdict: **silence**. The effusion source stays Damoiseau v2.

**The other six, baseline.** AUROC [95% CI by patient], with the number of
positives in each set.

| Finding | Own test | Reserve | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cardiomegaly | 0.915 [0.895, 0.932], n=380 | 0.899 [0.860, 0.933], n=75 | usable |
| Edema | 0.897 [0.875, 0.916], n=302 | 0.909 [0.877, 0.937], n=83 | usable |
| Pneumothorax | 0.856 [0.832, 0.877], n=629 | 0.849 [0.766, 0.906], n=170 | usable |
| Mass | 0.831 [0.809, 0.853], n=830 | 0.872 [0.831, 0.906], n=161 | usable |
| Consolidation | 0.808 [0.788, 0.827], n=689 | 0.772 [0.729, 0.810], n=144 | usable on test, weak on reserve |
| Nodule | 0.746 [0.721, 0.768], n=939 | 0.692 [0.641, 0.740], n=196 | weak |

No headline finding reached 0.95. The seven secondary labels (atelectasis,
infiltration, pneumonia, emphysema, fibrosis, pleural thickening, hernia) are
trained and reported, but carry no verdict. Calibration held up: the calibrated
Brier score for effusion on the reserve is 0.0754, against 0.0756 for both
Damoiseau seeds.

Setup: NIH ChestX-ray14, 112,120 frontal radiographs, split by patient. Own
test set 15,787 images from 4,466 patients; reserve 3,869 images from 1,036
patients. Kaggle T4, 177 minutes in total, five epochs of about 27 minutes.
The mean validation AUROC of the seven peaked at epoch 4 (0.853) and fell at
epoch 5, so more epochs were not the obvious next step.

## What I got wrong

**The effusion question was sharper than the ruler.** Against the first
Damoiseau seed, Abreu's lower bound (−0.0120) crosses the −0.010 margin, so
the result is not "preserved". Against the second seed it is −0.0003, so it is
not "lost" either. The gap to the first seed, −0.0072, is the same size as the
gap between the two seeds of Damoiseau itself, −0.0068. With two control
seeds, the comparison cannot resolve anything finer than what changes from one
seed to the next. The margin was set without enough measurements of seed noise
to know that.

**The operating point does not travel.** Each finding's threshold was chosen on
validation to catch at least 90% of positives. On pneumothorax it catches 82%,
on both the own test set and the reserve, with 629 and 170 positives. That is
not sampling noise. On the reserve, cardiomegaly, consolidation and nodule land
at 84–85%. So no threshold from NIH goes anywhere labelled "sensitivity 0.9".
Any new site has to recompute its own operating point on its own data.

**A floor that fired without anyone hearing it.** The pre-registered effusion
rule asks whether the model's most confident calls reach a positive predictive
value of 0.80. On a thin slice of validation they did, at 0.82, just as they
had for both Damoiseau seeds. Read at the coverage v1 was judged at, it drops to
0.74. That is tail noise, not a property of the model, and every positive
sentence keeps its human-review flag. The run's own log did not flag the
drop: the code compared the floor with the wrong zone of predictions. The
adversarial review caught it, and the fix went into the next commit.

**Known and unmeasured.** In NIH, many pneumothorax-positive films already
have a chest drain in place. A good pneumothorax AUROC may be partly a good
drain detector. Without drain annotations, that is declared here rather than
measured.

**One rule was written after the result.** After v1, a finding counts as a
target only if it is *usable* on both held-out sets. That rule was written with
these numbers in view, so it applies from the next round on and does not
reclassify this one.

## Next

   Each is pre-registered against this run and its seeds, and together they get
